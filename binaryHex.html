03 / Part 1

Move the bits. Watch the edges.

A logical shift moves every bit left or right. Empty positions are filled with 0. Any bits that pass the edge of our eight-bit space are discarded.

Left by n: multiply by 2ⁿ if the result fits.
Right by n: divide by 2ⁿ and discard the fractional part.

04 / Part 2

Sixteen digits. The same numbers.

Hexadecimal (hex) is base 16. It uses 0–9, then A–F for 10–15. In a two-digit hex number, the left digit counts 16s and the right digit counts 1s.

Hex 10 means denary 16. Read it as “one zero” to avoid mixing the bases.

Convert denary to hex

Divide by 16. The whole-number quotient gives the first digit; the remainder gives the second. For 44: 44 = 2 × 16 + 12 → 2C.

Your turn: convert denary 59 to two-digit hex.

Show the working

59 = 3 × 16 + 11. 11 is B, so the result is 3B.

05 / Part 2

One nibble. One hex digit.

Split eight bits into two groups of four. Convert each nibble separately and keep their order. You do not have to convert the whole byte to denary first.

Each group has local place values 8, 4, 2, 1. Keep four bits per hex digit: hex 03 becomes 0000 0011.

Try the reverse

Convert hexadecimal 1E into eight bits. Spaces between groups are fine.

Show the working

1 = 0001. E = 14 = 1110. Join the groups: 00011110.

Optional: a little colour connection

A colour code can use three pairs of hex digits. Here the middle pair comes from your switches, while the other pairs stay at 00 and FF. You do not need to learn colour encoding today.

Hex is shorter for people to read and write: six digits instead of 24 bits for the same value. It does not compress the underlying data.

Covers unsigned 8-bit addition, overflow, logical shifts and conversions among binary, denary and two-digit hexadecimal. OCR J277 specification.

The tool distinguishes full mathematical sums from retained low bits. Left shifts multiply exactly only when significant bits are not lost. Right shifts discard any fractional part for these unsigned integers. Carrying within a byte does not itself imply overflow. Signed numbers, subtraction and detailed colour encoding are outside scope.
